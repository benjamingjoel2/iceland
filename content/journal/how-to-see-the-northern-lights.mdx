The aurora borealis is one of the great natural spectacles, and Iceland sits beneath the auroral oval. But it is never guaranteed. Here is what actually matters.

## 1. Darkness

You need a properly dark sky. In Iceland that means roughly September to April, when nights are long. In midsummer the sky never gets dark enough. Within the season, the further you are from towns and their light, the better.

## 2. Clear skies

This matters more than anything else. A strong aurora behind thick cloud is invisible. Iceland's weather moves quickly, and cloud often breaks in one region while another is overcast. This is why we **follow the clear sky**, not a fixed viewpoint: your guide watches the cloud forecasts and drives to wherever the gaps are.

## 3. Solar activity

The aurora is caused by charged particles from the sun meeting the upper atmosphere. Forecasts of geomagnetic activity help, but even a quiet night can surprise you. We check them, but we never stay in because of them.

## 4. Time

The single best way to improve your chances is to stay longer. Five nights or more in the dark months gives you several chances at a clear night.

## How we plan for it

- Lodges chosen for dark skies, with aurora wake-up calls on request
- A guide who watches the forecasts and will drive you to clearer skies
- Blankets, hot drinks and, if you wish, a photographer who will help with your camera settings

### Photography in brief

A tripod, a wide lens, manual focus set to infinity and exposures of a few seconds. Your guide will help on the night.
