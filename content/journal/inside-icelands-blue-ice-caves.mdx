Deep inside the outlet glaciers of Vatnajökull, meltwater carves tunnels through ice that may be centuries old. In winter, when the glaciers are cold and stable, some of these become accessible. Step inside and the light changes: the ice is so dense and free of air that it absorbs every colour except blue.

## Why only in winter

Natural ice caves are typically visited from **November to March**. In warmer months meltwater flows through them and the ice is unstable. Even in winter, conditions change constantly, and every cave is different each year: the one you visit has never existed before and will not exist again.

## Safety first, always

We work only with **certified ice cave guides**, who inspect the caves daily and decide whether they are safe to enter. If they say no, we do not go. That is non-negotiable.

- Helmets, crampons and equipment are provided
- Your guide will brief you on the glacier and the cave
- Visits can be moved within your stay if conditions are poor

> The cave you stand in has never existed before, and next winter it will be gone.

## Pairing it with the aurora

Our winter journey spends several nights beneath the glaciers, so that blue days in the ice can be followed by nights watching for the aurora.
